# Shrimp Cultivation in Bangladesh: An Adaptation Strategy or a Maladaptation Strategy?

A review paper written for **ENVS4004: Global Climate Change** at the Asian University for Women, by Nahian Ibnat.

**Website:** https://nahianibnat-2000.github.io/ShrimpCultivation_ReviewPaper/

## About the paper

Shrimp farming became one of Bangladesh's biggest export industries, growing from about 20,000 hectares in 1980 to about 244,000 hectares in 2014. It supports more than two million people, but it has also been linked to salt intrusion, loss of crops and fish, water pollution, mangrove destruction and growing inequality on the southwest coast.

The paper reviews the existing research to answer two questions:

1. Is shrimp cultivation an adaptation strategy or a maladaptation?
2. Is it possible to cultivate shrimp for export in Bangladesh sustainably?

### Main findings

- Shrimp farming brings short-term gains for rich farmers and the government, but the poor bear most of the environmental and social costs.
- Salt water in ponds degrades soil and water quality and displaces local crops and fish.
- Converting mangroves to ponds releases stored blue carbon and removes natural storm protection.
- In the long run, the paper expects even the current winners to lose as buyers turn to sustainable producers.
- Integrated Coastal Zone Management (ICZM) is the main proposed route to sustainability, alongside rain harvesting, mangrove restoration, salt-tolerant crops and new shrimp species such as Pacific white shrimp.

**Keywords:** maladaptation, aquaculture, shrimp farming, sustainability, ICZM

## Methodology in brief

Sources were found through Google Scholar, SAGE and Cambridge Core. They had to be peer-reviewed primary research published from 2000 onward and relevant to shrimp cultivation, climate change and adaptation.
